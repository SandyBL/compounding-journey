Five simulators

Financial simulators

Five tools for practising decisions before you make them with real money. All free, all running in your browser, none of them asking you to sign up.

Decision trainer

Personal Finance Simulator

It puts the ordinary money choices of a life in front of you — a raise, a car, a holiday, an unexpected bill, a market fall — and shows what each one does to four things at once: your wealth, your cash flow, your reported wellbeing and your financial knowledge.

Who it is for: For anyone who understands the maths and still cannot say why they decide what they decide.

A date, not a total

Freedom Calendar

It turns your income, your spending and your existing investments into a single date: the day you stop needing a salary. Then it restates each recurring expense in the unit it actually costs, which is days of work.

Who it is for: For anyone already saving who wants to know when they arrive, not how much they pile up.

A century of real markets

Market Time Machine

You design a portfolio and run it through the returns that actually happened from 1920 onward: the Great Depression, the war years, 1970s inflation, the dot-com collapse, 2008, the pandemic. Not a projection — what your allocation would have lived through.

Who it is for: For anyone who has never watched their own portfolio go through a crash.

Probability, not an average

Monte Carlo FIRE

It runs your withdrawal plan a thousand times, drawing a different sequence of market years each time, and returns a probability instead of a number: the share of those futures in which the money lasts.

Who it is for: For anyone with a target figure who wants to know how much margin is left in it.

Income, stream by stream

Passive Income Engine

You build an income stream out of six different sources — dividends, rent, interest, a business, royalties, a portfolio — and then try to live on it, until the differences between them stop being an idea and turn into a bill.

Who it is for: For anyone who hears "passive income" as though it were one thing.

Where to start

If you arrive without a specific question, start with the Personal Finance Simulator: it asks you for nothing and shows you a decision moving within two minutes. The other four each answer one narrow question, so if yours is above, go straight there.

What happens to what you type

The maths happens entirely in your browser: nothing you type is sent anywhere while you play. Three of the simulators keep a public ranking and the other two carry a button that contributes your run, and in both cases something is only sent when you press it. What is contributed appears aggregated, without a name, on the results page.
