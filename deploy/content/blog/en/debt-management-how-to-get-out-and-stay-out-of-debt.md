## Understanding Good Debt vs. Bad Debt

Not all debt is created equal[cite: 1]. Borrowing to acquire assets that appreciate or increase earning potential can build wealth, whereas financing consumer items drags you down[cite: 1].

| Debt Type | Category | Typical Interest Rate | Impact on Wealth |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Student Loans** | Good Debt[cite: 1] | Low to Moderate | Boosts lifetime earning potential[cite: 1] |

## Two Proven Strategies for Paying Off Debt

If you carry multiple balances, choosing an intentional repayment strategy accelerates your timeline[cite: 1].

### 1. The Snowball Method (Best for Motivation)
* **How it works:** Pay off the debt with the smallest balance first while making minimum payments on all others[cite: 1]. Once cleared, roll that entire monthly payment into the next smallest balance[cite: 1].
* **Why it works:** Quick wins create psychological momentum that keeps you committed[cite: 1].

### 2. The Avalanche Method (Best for Saving Money)
* **How it works:** Direct extra cash toward the debt with the highest interest rate first while paying minimums on the rest[cite: 1].
* **Why it works:** Mathematically minimizes total interest paid, saving you the most money over time[cite: 1].

---

## Tips to Avoid Common Debt Traps

* **Avoid Minimum-Only Payments:** Paying only the minimum on credit cards keeps you trapped in high-interest debt for years[cite: 1].
* **Negotiate Lower Interest Rates:** Call your lenders and request a lower APR based on your payment history[cite: 1].
* **Build a Starter Emergency Fund:** Having $500 to $1,000 set aside prevents relying on credit when unexpected expenses arise[cite: 1].
